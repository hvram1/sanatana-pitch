How a new book reaches sanatana.in · the Smṛtimuktāphalam

Agents do the counting. People look at the pages.

The Smṛtimuktāphalam is Vaidyanātha Dīkṣita's digest of dharma. We have it in a Tamil edition that sets each Sanskrit passage above its Tamil rendering: seven parts, 4,333 scanned pages. Between 4 and 17 September 2026 it became a searchable edition at hvram1.github.io/smp, linked both ways with Kane's History of Dharmaśāstra. This page follows it from the scans to the site.

Agents dosurvey the scans; propose what each page is; read the text in cheap batches and keep a record of what was paid for; find the scan's own faults; check the book against itself; build the edition and link it to what we already hold.
People decidewhich book and which edition, and on what terms we use the scans; what each doubtful page is; whether the transcribed text can be trusted. A vidvān has not yet read it, and the page says so.

What happens

Eight steps, three of them decided by people

Round markers are work an agent does by itself. Diamonds are where the work stops and waits for a person. Each step is marked with where it stands today.

  1. 1

    TeamChoose the book, and settle the terms done

    The scans come from Musiri's collection and belong to svradhakrishnasastri.in. The decision was to publish the text we read from them and the pages built from that text, and never the scans. The scans are not copied into any repository.

    ResultSeven PDFs on a drive, used where they are, and a clear line on what may be published.
  2. 2

    AgentLook at the scans before writing anything done

    The agent asks the PDFs what they hold before building anything on them. Every text page is one black-and-white image at 300 dpi. The colour pages are the portraits of the Ācāryas, the covers, and one ordinary text page that was scanned in colour by accident. The contents pages give themselves away by their dot leaders, as in दानयोग्यः - தானயோக்யர்கள் ......... 200.

    ResultThe contents pages of all seven parts found. Each row gives the section title in Sanskrit and Tamil and the page it starts on: the book's own map of itself.
  3. 3

    Team memberSay what each doubtful page is done

    The agent proposes a class for every page (text, contents, front matter, picture or blank) and lays all of them out as a sheet of thumbnails. A person goes down the sheet and corrects it. Two pages show why no rule can be trusted alone. Part 5 page 44 is in colour, but it is text. Part 5 page 29 is a contents page whose entries all wrap onto a second line, so it shows too few dot leaders for any rule to catch.

    ResultEvery page has a class a person has seen. A correction stays in place even if the agent's rule changes later, and nothing is deleted: a page called "picture" is set aside, because the class is a judgement and the scan is the only copy.
  4. 4

    AgentRead the text: a pilot first, then in bulk done

    Eleven pages are read first and checked. Then the agent sends the rest to Gemini in batches of 300 pages, which costs half as much as reading page by page and can take a day or more. Each kind of page has its own prompt, so contents pages, front matter and running text are each read for what they are.

    Result37 paid batch jobs, each written to a ledger as it is sent. The ledger is what stops a paid batch being sent twice, and it is kept in the repository because nothing can rebuild it.
  5. 5

    AgentCheck what came back, and find the scan's own faults done

    For every part the agent looks for missing pages, replies that stop short, and printed page numbers that do not go up one at a time. Those checks found the faults in the table above. One leaf of Part 5 was sent back twice, and both times the model replied in plain words that the image was upside down. Now the check reads that reply and stops it from being sent a third time.

    ResultEach fault is declared once, and every later step reads the declaration. Part 4 had no single page offset that fitted until its duplicate leaves and its missing page were declared. After that one offset fitted 320 of its 320 leaves.

    Why this matters: a citation into this edition is by printed page. If the leaf-to-page map is wrong, every link into the book lands on the wrong page and looks right while doing it.

  6. 6

    AgentCheck the book against itself done

    The contents list was typeset separately from the text it points to. Where the body prints a heading, that heading is a second witness to where the section starts. The agent puts the two page numbers side by side.

    Contents entry against the bodySections
    Confirmed: the heading stands on the stated page162
    Contradicted: the same title, four or more pages away2
    Could not be checked: the body prints no heading there144

    One of the two: the contents list puts शिवपूजाप्रशंसा on page 249, and the body prints that heading on page 242. Nothing is corrected. The contents list is the book's statement about itself, so it stays as printed, and the disagreement is shown beside the section.

  7. 7

    AgentBuild the edition, and link it to what we already hold done

    The text goes into one database. From it come one page per kāṇḍa and an anukramaṇikā, a register of every section across all seven. Every quotation records whom the digest credits. Then the edition is joined to Kane, in both directions, and every built page is opened in a browser and checked against the database before it is published.

    Result
    • 4,909 quotations, 1,303 of them credited to a work rather than a person.
    • 407 of Kane's 9,434 Sanskrit footnotes found in the digest or a Purāṇa, with a control of 1.
    • Kane's 69 mentions of the digest now open the edition, at the kāṇḍa.

    Why at the kāṇḍa and not the page: Kane cites the pages of the printing he had. He says the digest quotes Ṛgveda I.154.5 at "āhnika p. 390". That verse occurs once in our edition, on page 231. A link built from his page number would land on an unrelated page and look right.

  8. 8

    VidvānRead a sample against the scans not started

    The checks so far are about structure: pages, order, sections, links. None of them says whether the Sanskrit and the Tamil were read correctly, letter by letter. That needs a person who reads both, with the scan beside the text, as in the Yajurveda review.

    Until thenThe edition is published as a machine reading. Corrections from readers go through the correction loop.
The human stays in control

What the agents are never allowed to do

  • Publish or copy the scans. They belong to their owner. Only the text read from them is published.
  • Delete a page. A page judged a picture or a blank is set aside, never removed.
  • Renumber or move a scanned file. A fault in the scan is declared, so "page 437" always means the 437th image of the PDF.
  • Correct the book's account of itself. Where the contents list and the body disagree, both are shown.
  • Judge an attribution. Where the digest credits a verse to Yājñavalkya and Kane to the Ṛgveda, both credits are shown as each source gives them.
  • Link by a number that belongs to another printing, or publish a link that has not been checked against the target's own index.
  • Send a paid batch twice. Every job is in the ledger before it is sent.

Questions before the next book

  • Who reads the sample in step 8: one vidvān for the Sanskrit and another for the Tamil?
  • Does the agreement with svradhakrishnasastri.in cover the published OCR text, as well as the pages built from it? This should be written down.
  • Which book is next, and does it have a printed contents list? This one had a map of itself, and much of the checking leaned on it.
  • Three items in the edition's work plan are queued: showing how sure each quotation's end is, the 22 quotations given twice, and the 47 sections that cite nobody. Do they come before the next book?
Appendix · for the engineers

How it is built

The pipeline is dharmasastra-gcp/smp/: twenty stages from extraction to the site, described in smp/README.md, with the queued work in smp/WORKPLAN.md. Where they and this page differ, they win.

  • Extract and sort. extract_all.sh renames pdfimages output by page rather than by image, because Paper Capture leaves stencils that shift the numbering by 856 by the end of Part 1. smp_classify.py writes the manifest and the contact sheet, and keeps a person's class over a later proposal. smp_sort.py moves pages into folders by class and records the sha1 of each turned leaf.
  • Read. smp_ocr.py sends 300-page batch chunks and writes each job to smp_jobs.tsv. smp_check.py grades the replies and writes the re-run list, keeping a ROTATED page off it.
  • Scan faults. SCAN_ROTATE, SCAN_DUPLICATE, SCAN_MISSING and SCAN_ORDER in smp_books.py. reading_order() is what everything downstream orders by.
  • Build. smp_build.py runs smp_db, smp_twolists, smp_concord, smp_site, smp_verify and smp_publish in that order, and reruns whatever is stale. The order matters, and a broken order fails silently. smp_verify.py opens every page in a browser and asserts against the database.
  • Publish. smp_export.py writes ../smp/, its own repository, which GitHub Pages serves. It adds the doctype and <meta charset>; without them a served page decodes as Latin-1 and every letter is garbled.

How a fault or a request becomes a change · the Smṛtimuktāphalam on a phone

A report is evidence. A fix is measured before it ships.

The people who use these texts are mostly on phones: purohits, and older readers for whom the phone is the working machine, not a second screen. On 17 September 2026 someone using the new Smṛtimuktāphalam edition reported that links into it did nothing. This page follows that report through four fixes. One of them is a new feature.

Agents doreproduce the fault at phone width; measure where things are; rule causes out one by one; write the smallest change that removes the fault; check that nothing else moved; look for the same fault elsewhere.
People decidewhat counts as broken, since the person who reports a fault has seen it; what a real phone shows when the agent's browser does not; whether the change is right for readers; when it ships.
The report

"I click a link and nothing happens"

The link did work. On a phone the edition stacks its two panes: the list of the kāṇḍa's 193 sections comes first, and the section the link names comes after it. Section 42 was drawn correctly, 15,850 pixels down a page 21,094 pixels long. The reader was left at the top, looking at a list.

What happened next

Seven steps, three of them people's

Round markers are work an agent does by itself. Diamonds are where a person saw, decided or supplied something the agent could not. All seven steps took place on 17 September.

  1. 1

    ReporterReport what they saw done

    "I click a link and nothing happens." It is not a technical description, and it does not need to be. It says what the reader did and what they expected.

  2. 2

    AgentReproduce it at phone width, and measure done

    The agent loads the page in a browser 390 pixels wide and reads back where things are drawn. The section is there, 15,850 pixels down. Of the three kinds of address the edition publishes, only quotation links scrolled. That is how the fault survived: whoever had checked a link had checked that kind.

    Fix 1Scroll to the thing named, and only when it is off screen. On a desktop, where the panes sit side by side, nothing moves. The section and authority links among the anukramaṇikā's 1,117 addresses, dead on a phone until then, now land in the test browser.
  3. 3

    Reporter"It still does nothing" done

    On a real phone, the fix had not fixed it. The agent's browser and the reporter's phone disagreed, and the phone was right.

  4. 4

    AgentRule out causes one by one done

    The live page matches the build byte for byte, there are no script errors, the fonts do not move it, and the data loads in 13 ms. So the fault is in something a phone browser does and a test browser does not. There are three such things.

    Fix 2
    • A smooth scroll of 15,000 pixels takes seconds on a phone, and any touch cancels it. Past one screen the scroll is now instant.
    • On reload or back, the browser puts back its remembered position after the page has landed. When the address names something, the address now wins.
    • A Devanagari font that arrives late reflows a list 15,000 pixels long. The landing is checked again once the fonts are in.
  5. 5

    ReporterSend a screenshot from the phone done

    The screenshot showed what the agent's instruments could not. The page had honoured the address exactly: the right row was highlighted, third in the list. The reader was at the top of that list, and the section itself was fifteen thousand pixels further down. The scroll that should have taken them there had been cancelled by their own finger.

    This is the step no amount of testing replaced. One picture from the device in the reporter's hand settled what the measuring had circled round.

  6. 6

    AgentStop making the journey, then give the way back done

    Making the scroll faster treated the symptom. When the address names something, that thing now comes first, so there is no journey to make. That fixed arriving and broke leaving: to pick another section, the reader now had to scroll past the whole section, 845 passages in the longest, to reach the list below it.

    Fix 3, which is a featureOn a phone the list and the section are two views, and only one is on screen at a time. A button fixed at the bottom switches between them: "☰ All 193 sections of smp1" while reading, "Read this section अध्यापनम्" while browsing. Picking a section from the list is itself the way back. On a desktop nothing changes.
  7. 7

    AgentCheck the whole round trip, then publish done

    At 390 pixels, with the fonts loading: arrive at §42, switch to the list, pick §8, land on §8. The full test suite passes on all seven kāṇḍas with no overflow. The desktop is checked to be unchanged. Each fix went out as its own change, with the measurements in its description.

The same loop, the same day

One report, then a search for its relatives

A fault reported once is rarely the only one of its kind. Once the first report was fixed, the agents went looking for the same classes of fault elsewhere, and a suggestion from the maintainer went through the same steps as a fault.

Kane's book had never been opened on a phoneLoaded at 390 pixels, page type by page type, it showed four faults. Reference tables could not shrink below 828 pixels. A 59-letter Devanagari compound held every chapter 118 pixels too wide. The menu bar did not wrap. One page of three prose columns overflowed. Sixteen pages across every type now measure 0 pixels of overflow.Found by loading each page in a real browser and reading back what stuck out, not by reading the styles.
103 links that all went nowhereEvery link from the edition's concordance into Kane's book pointed at an address where the book is not published. All 103 shipped dead, and nothing noticed, because nothing checked that a published address leads somewhere. Now a check resolves every such link against the files about to be published.A web status code proves nothing here: both sites answer "found" for any address beneath them.
A suggestion that beat the plan"Put the verse's whereabouts on the footnote." The agent had placed sources only where it could parse Kane's citation. Matching the verse in the footnote instead, against every verse we hold, raised the count from 197 references placed to 611 footnotes, with 326 links and a control of 0.A person's idea, measured before it shipped, like a fix.
The human stays in control

What the agents are never allowed to do

  • Close a report because they cannot reproduce it. When the agent's browser and a reporter's phone disagree, the phone is right.
  • Make a wrong link look right. An old link that built Mahābhārata addresses from verse numbers was retired, not repaired. Repaired, it would have landed on whatever verse carries that number in our recension, which is worse than a dead link.
  • Trust a status code as proof. A link is checked against the target site's own index of what it publishes.
  • Fix one screen by moving another. Every phone fix is checked to leave the desktop unchanged.
  • Ship a change without its measurements, or bundle unrelated work into it. Each fix says what it changed and how that was checked.
How we will know it works

What to measure from here

Report to fix, liveTime from the first report to a fix on the reporter's phone. This one needed three tries, all on 17 September.
Reports that come back"It still does nothing." Each time that happens is a fix that was checked in the wrong place.
Faults found before a reader finds themOverflow at phone width and dead links, caught by the checks before publishing rather than reported after.
For the team to decide

Questions before building

  • Where do reports come in: a button on every page, WhatsApp, or both? Who reads them first?
  • Should a real phone be part of the check before every publish? The screenshot in step 5 is the argument for one.
  • Who tells the reader that their report is fixed, and how? Nothing in this story records that it was done.
  • Which requests are features for the team to weigh, rather than faults for an agent to fix? The two-view switch began as a fix and changed how the edition works on a phone.

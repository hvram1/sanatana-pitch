Purāṇa Nāma-Kośa · पुराणनामकोशः · how it gets built

Agents do the legwork. Scholars decide.

The Nāma-Kośa will be an index of every person and place in the eighteen Purāṇas, each tied to the verses that name them. Building it means careful reading of 351,225 verses. This page follows one request from start to finish, to show which parts software agents can take on and where a person has to decide.

Agents dothe searching, sorting and preparing; handing out reading; reminders; comparing answers; rebuilding the website; telling people what changed.
People decidewho a name in a verse refers to; whether two people are the same; whether two tellings differ. A vidvān signs every entry, and nothing reaches the website before that.
The request

A student asks about Yayāti

A pāṭhaśālā student is asked: "Is there a character whose story you would like to tell?" The answer is Yayāti, the king who traded his old age for his son's youth. He matters well beyond his own story: his son Pūru's line leads to the Kauravas, the Pāṇḍavas and Parīkṣit, and his story is told again in the Bhāgavata and other Purāṇas.

Here is his family as our index holds it today. Switch to After to see what it should look like once the work is done. The rest of the page is how it gets from one to the other.

What happens next

Seven steps, three of them decided by people

Round markers are work an agent does by itself. Diamonds are where the work stops and waits for a person.

  1. 1

    AgentThe request is logged

    An agent opens one job for Yayāti and his whole family. If someone later asks about Devayānī or Pūru, the request joins this job instead of starting the work again.

    ResultOne job covering 27 people.
  2. 2

    AgentCheck what we already have

    Before anyone reads a verse, an agent looks up what the index already holds about these 27 people. A great deal is there, copied from printed indexes, but no scholar has checked any of it.

    Result: a checklist in three parts
    • Settled: nothing yet. No scholar has ruled on anyone in this family.
    • Known but unchecked: 36 family links from Sørensen's index of the Mahābhārata, and 220 Mahābhārata verses cited for Yayāti.
    • Missing: about 477 Purāṇa verses that contain his name, none of them linked to him.
    It also flags what looks wrong
    • Three links join the wrong person of the same name: there are 11 Janamejayas in the index, and a Janamejaya "son of Kuru" was linked to this one.
    • Devayānī is recorded as Vṛṣaparvan's daughter. She is Śukra's.
    • His Purāṇa verses were filed under Yayātipatana, a place named after him.
    • Śantanu is stored under three spellings.

    Why this step comes first: nobody rereads what is already settled, and nobody trusts a link just because it is there.

  3. 3

    Junior vidvānCheck the known links

    A junior vidvān goes through the 36 known links as a list, ticking the right ones and striking the wrong ones, such as the links to the wrong Janamejaya and Devayānī's wrong father. That is a short sitting, not a reading of the Mahābhārata.

    ResultThe Mahābhārata side of the family is checked and signed. Only the Purāṇa verses still need reading.
  4. 4

    AgentFind the verses and hand them out

    An agent searches all eighteen Purāṇas for every form of his name, and for Nāhuṣa ("son of Nahuṣa"), which also refers to him. It sorts what it finds so that students read stories as stories, not as scattered lines. It then sends batches of twenty verses to two students each. A few verses in every batch already have known answers, which checks the reading.

    The first trial of this search made mistakes, which is why people read what it finds. It put two Kṛṣṇa chapters among Yayāti's stories because they mention Yadu and Śukra, and शुक्र can also mean the planet. It also counted a verse where "son of Nāhuṣa" means Yadu, Yayāti's son.

  5. 5

    Two studentsRead each verse, separately

    On a phone, each student answers one question per verse: is this our Yayāti? They throw out false matches, such as शिबिका (a palanquin) caught by a search for Śibi, or देवयान (the path of the gods) caught by a search for Devayānī.

    Meanwhile an agent keeps things moving. If a batch sits untouched, it sends a reminder after three days and passes the batch to another student after seven. When both students are done, it compares their answers.

    ResultEach verse has two independent answers, either in agreement or not.
  6. 6

    VidvānsDecide and sign

    Where the two students agree, a junior vidvān confirms and signs. Where they disagree, or the question is hard, it goes to the senior vidvān, with the evidence laid out on one page by an agent. The senior vidvān sees only these.

    The kind of question that reaches the senior vidvān
    • The Garuḍa Purāṇa says that by hearing it, Kaśyapa revived a burnt tree. Is that the Kāśyapa of the Mahābhārata who revives the tree Takṣaka burns, on his way to save Parīkṣit? Same person, different person, or cannot tell?
    • The four kings in Virāṭa Parva 56.9: are they Mādhavī's sons?
    • The Bhāgavata tells Parīkṣit's death differently from the Mahābhārata. Both tellings are kept, side by side. Neither is marked "not in the original".

    ResultEvery verse decided and signed. Nothing goes further without a signature.
  7. 7

    AgentPublish, and say who did the work

    Each signed decision is recorded with the names of the students who read it and the vidvān who signed it. Overnight the website is rebuilt from these records. Yayāti's page now lists his verses in each Purāṇa, and the map above becomes the After picture. Each student gets a WhatsApp message: "Your verses on Bhāgavata 9.18 are live." An upanyāsakar is invited to tell the story from the signed verses.

The human stays in control

What the agents are never allowed to do

  • Decide who a name refers to. An agent can suggest. Only a student, then a vidvān, can decide.
  • Publish anything unsigned. The website is rebuilt only from signed decisions, and the rebuild refuses a page that points to an unsigned one.
  • Settle a disagreement, even an obvious one. Disagreements go to a person.
  • Call one telling wrong. When two texts tell a story differently, both are kept and shown side by side.
  • Overwrite a mistake. A correction is a new signed entry. The old one stays visible, so anyone can see what changed and who changed it.
  • Let suggestions do the reading. Some verses in every batch are shown with no suggestion. If students only agree with the agent when they can see its suggestion, the suggestions are switched off.
  • Show a student's name without consent. Many readers are minors, so a name appears only with their consent and their pāṭhaśālā's. Students are credited for reading even when they are overruled. There are no leaderboards.
How we will know it works

Try it on the Harivaṃśa first, half with agents and half without

The Harivaṃśa pilot covers 16,100 verses and up to 4,034 names, over 9 months, for ₹37.5 lakh. Nearly all of that cost is people's reading time. Running half the chapters with agents and half without shows whether the agents save that time, before they are used on all 351,225 verses.

For the team to decide

Questions before building

  • How is consent for a minor's name recorded, and by whom: the student, a parent, or the pāṭhaśālā?
  • Should two students read every verse, or only the ones the agent is unsure of? Two readers doubles the students' time, but it is the only way to know how often they agree.
  • How many verses in each batch should go without a suggestion? One in five is a starting guess.
  • Which senior vidvān signs for the Harivaṃśa, and how many hours a month can they give? That sets the pace more than anything else.
  • Which upanyāsakars would take up a request to tell a figure's story from the signed verses, and how is that request made?
Appendix · for the engineers

How it is built

This follows the Scholar Workbench design from August 2026 (How the index gets made). Where this page and that design differ, the design wins.

The agents

  • Each agent is a Claude Code run in its own sandbox, started by an event (a request, an upload, a signed decision) or on a schedule. Agents write only to the work queue and the daily report. Only signed decisions reach the index.
  • Decisions are a log that only grows. Each signed decision is one record: the verse, the name as written, the person it refers to, who read it, who signed it, when. Printed sources (Sørensen, the Gītā Press index) are never overwritten. A ruling has three possible outcomes: this person, not this person, or both readings stand.
  • Pages are never edited by hand. People write in four places only: the reading page on the phone, the senior vidvān's notes, one discussion thread per name, and code. Everything on the website is rebuilt from those. If a page is wrong, the decision behind it is wrong.
  • Build order. Two things come first because they cannot be added later: saving decisions from a phone with an unreliable connection, and the nightly rebuild with its report. With about ten readers, a spreadsheet can assign the work and a person can do the chasing. The agents take over from those later.
  • No accounts. Each reader gets a private link. Readers use a phone page that works offline, and WhatsApp. They never see GitHub or code.

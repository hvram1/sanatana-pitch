Purāṇa Nāma-Kośa · पुराणनामकोशः · a working sketch

One story, followed from the Mahābhārata into the Purāṇas

Say "Yayāti", and agents light up everyone tied to him: his gurus, his queens, his sons, his daughter, and Pūru's line down to Parīkṣit and Janamejaya. From there, two doors lead into the Purāṇas. This page shows that map, then what happens to it as work arrives. Agents run the loop. Students, junior vidvāns and a senior vidvān make every judgement.

Agents maylight up a circle of people, find candidate verses, build dossiers, assign and chase work, compare readers, rebuild the site, and tell people what changed.
Only a person maysay who a name stands for in a verse, merge or split an identity, decide that two tellings differ, write an entry, or sign.
This builds on the Scholar Workbench. Its design, written in August, already settles the log, the roles, the dossier, the nightly build and the rules for credit: How the index gets made (published), and the fuller design note at dharmasastra-gcp/workbench/docs/DESIGN-scholar-workbench-2026-08-21.md. This page adds one layer: agents that run the coordination the note says no software settles. Where the two differ, the note wins.
1 · The map · Arc 2 of Mūla on Stage

Yayāti's world, and two doors into the Purāṇas

The relations come from Sørensen's index, already in the corpus as son of, spouse of, daughter of. So the agent does not guess who belongs together; it reads it. The badges show where the same people stand in the Bhāgavata and the Garuḍa Purāṇa, found by reading the chapter headings of the Śrī Śāradā Pīṭham text.

Open the map on its ownIts source is namakosa/map.py in the pitch repository.

Door 1 · the Bhāgavata, through Parīkṣit

The whole of Arcs 1 and 2 in one Purāṇa: Yayāti in 9.18–19, Pūru's line in 9.20 and 9.22, the curse in 1.18, Śuka's coming in 1.19, and the snake sacrifice in 12.6. The frame mirrors the epic's: the father hears the Bhāgavata waiting for Takṣaka; the son hears the Mahābhārata at the sacrifice held to avenge him.

Door 2 · the Garuḍa Purāṇa, through Garuḍa

Not a retelling of the wager. Kadrū appears once, Āstīka and the sacrifice not at all. Garuḍa is the one who tells this Purāṇa (Ācārakāṇḍa 2). But Ācārakāṇḍa 3 is headed यच्छ्रवणात् कश्यपो दग्धं वृक्षमजीवयत्: by hearing it, Kaśyapa revived a burnt tree. That is the tree Takṣaka burns on his way to Parīkṣit. Both doors meet at his death.

Counts on the map are string matches, not people: पूरु also matches पूरुष, and गरुड matches every mention of Viṣṇu's mount. Chapter numbers for the Mahābhārata are the plan's working references, from Dutt.

2 · Discover before reading

Much of Yayāti's circle is already there. None of it is ruled.

This is the dossier of the design note's §6, made per circle instead of per headword: the graded shortlist, the corpus's own chapter headings, who else stands where he stands, and the frontier where evidence stops.

Every item starts with a look at what the index already holds, so no one rereads a verse that is settled, and no one trusts a link just because it exists. This is what that look finds today for the 27 people on the map (the Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas are groups), measured in the corpus database.

confirmed0

Scholar-adjudicated rulings on anyone in the circle. The index holds 308 such rulings in all (616 rows, each a +1 on the person who stands and a −1 on the rival), none of them here yet.

known, not checked36

Pairs of people on the map already linked by a recorded relation, from Sørensen. Yayāti alone has 220 verse-level citations from the Gītā Press index and 178 verses resolved through Dutt's translation. A junior vidvān can confirm these from a list.

missing or misfiled~477

Purāṇa verses containing Yayāti's name. The scan linked none of them to him. Only this pile needs fresh reading.

What "already there" hides

  • Wrong people of the same name. Janamejaya has 11 homonyms and Mādhavī has 6. Three of the 36 relations join the wrong ones: a Janamejaya who is "son of Kuru", another who is "son of Pūru", and a Mādhavī who is a Janamejaya's wife.
  • Parsing slips. One recorded relation makes Devayānī the daughter of Vṛṣaparvan, read out of Sørensen's prose. She is Śukra's daughter.
  • Hits filed under the wrong entry. Yayāti's Purāṇa hits went to Yayātipatana, a place, and to the Yayāti episode. Fourteen Bhāgavata hits for देवयान went to Devayāna, the path of the gods; some may be Devayānī in an inflected form.
  • Different spellings. Śantanu is under Śāntanu in Sørensen, and under षन्तनु among the 2,074 Harivaṃśa names already drafted for the pilot.

4 · What happens to this map

Three kinds of work, each a real case from it

Lighting Yayāti's circle is a request. The scan has already made a mistake on Parīkṣit, which is a correction. The Garuḍa Purāṇa's burnt tree is a link waiting to be ruled. Pick one to see who does what.

Every step in full
    Where the students' list comes from · measured on the Purāṇa texts

    Step 7 reads what steps 3, 5 and 6 prepare

    1. Discover (3) supplies the Mahābhārata verses. Nobody searches for them: 220 verse-level Gītā Press citations, 67 Sørensen chapter citations and 178 Dutt-resolved verses are already in the index. They go to the junior vidvān as a list to confirm (4), not to the students.
    2. The candidate finder (5) searches the Purāṇas. The existing scan filed Yayāti's Purāṇa hits under a place, so it searches the Śāradā Pīṭham text afresh for every inflection of the stem ययात-, for his epithet नाहुष / नहुषात्मज (Sørensen records Nāhuṣa as the same person), and for place names built on his name, kept apart.
    3. The dispatcher (6) cuts batches by chapter. Verses come in order, with the chapter heading on top and the neighbouring verses beside each one, so 9.18 is read as a story. A few known-answer verses are mixed in, and two students get each batch.
    Kind of candidateVersesHow a student meets it
    In a chapter where his circle also appears168Read in order, as a story
    In a chapter headed वंश, a genealogy22Quick check
    A passing mention elsewhere285One verse at a time

    What the first run got wrong

    • The story tier is too loose. Bhāgavata 10.53 and 10.71 landed in it because Yadu and Śukra are named there, but they are Kṛṣṇa chapters, and शुक्र can be the planet. The rule should need two distinct people from the circle, leaving out common words.
    • An epithet can name his son. In Bhāgavata 11.7.36, नाहुषात्मज is said to Yadu, as the son of Nāhuṣa, that is of Yayāti. Only reading catches it.
    • The name alone misses about 9%. The 45 epithet-only verses would never be found by searching for ययाति.

    These rules are a first pass. The Harivaṃśa pilot tunes them: the known-answer verses show whether the story tier really is mostly him.

    5 · Publishing, and the reader's name

    A student sees their name on the verse they read

    Credit attaches to decisions. Every signed decision carries who read it and who ruled it, and the nightly build puts those names on the three pages the work lands on: the verse, the character's card, and the readers' page. Today the 308 scholar rulings in the corpus record only a batch, such as READ-KICAKA-P04, and not the people. That is the gap to close. The design note already promises the result: “a reader who signs 200 and stops keeps their name in the apparatus”.

    How a decision becomes the published kośa

    1. The student decides on the phone page. Each decision carries the reader and the time, and is saved verse by verse, so a lost session costs nothing.
    2. The vidvān signs. The signature adds the vidvān and the date. Nothing reaches the kośa without it.
    3. The publisher appends one ledger line per decision. Nothing is overwritten; a correction is a new line that supersedes the old one. 
      {"address":   "BHP:S09:C018:V018",
 "form":      "ययातिर्",
 "entity":    "inm:12607",
 "polarity":  1,
 "kind":      "stands",
 "read_by":   ["r:017", "r:042"],
 "ruled_by":  "v:003",
 "signed":    "2026-11-14",
 "batch":     "READ-YAYATI-BHP09",
 "supersedes": null}
      An example line. The ids point to a readers' register that holds names and consent.
    4. The nightly build loads the ledger into the corpus as scholar-adjudicated rulings, the grade that already outranks every printed index, with reader and ruler kept beside each one.
    5. The sites rebuild: the atlas, the character cards, the readers' page. Publish-and-verify refuses any page whose credit points at an unsigned decision.
    6. The student is told, on WhatsApp, with a link: "Your decisions on Bhāgavata 9.18 are live. Here is the first verse."

    Rules for credit

    • Sponsors stay off the verse; readers sit beside it. The funding page promises that no donor's name is printed on a verse. A reader's name lives in the apparatus, one tap from the verse, never in the text.
    • Consent first. Many readers will be minors. A name appears only with their consent and their pāṭhaśālā's. Without it, the credit reads as initials or as the pāṭhaśālā.
    • Credit for reading, not for agreeing. A reader whose call was overruled is still listed on that verse. Disagreement is how hard verses are found.
    • No leaderboards. The workbench note's rule is never to assess by volume, so the readers' page is alphabetical and shows no ranking.
    • Credit stays. A student who graduates keeps their name on every verse they read, and a superseded ruling keeps its names in the history.
    • Further places: the capsule made from a character names the students who read it, and the nightly report lists each day's signatures.
    6 · The agents

    Eight jobs, each with a line it does not cross

    Each is a Claude Code run in its own sandbox, started by an event (a pin, a vote, an upload, a signed decision) or by the clock. None of them holds a pen on the index itself. They write to the queue and the report; only signed decisions reach the ledger.

    What the workbench note left open

    The part no software settles is the part agents can carry

    The scholar-workbench note ends with a list it calls neither software nor solvable by it. Each item is coordination, not judgement. An agent can do the running; a named person still owns the rule.

    From the noteWhat the agent doesWhat a person still owns
    Who assigns the workDispatcher hands out batches by calibration score, script and language, keeps two readers per verse, never gives a reader their own earlier verse to re-check.The junior vidvān sets who is cleared to read which Purāṇa.
    What happens when a reader stallsChaser sends a gentle reminder on WhatsApp after three days, and returns the batch to the pool after seven. Nothing is lost: work is saved verse by verse.Nobody is dropped by an agent. A stall twice running goes to the junior vidvān as a note.
    Who chases an unanswered escalationChaser keeps a clock on each escalation and resends it with the dossier on top, then lists it in the nightly report until it is ruled.The senior vidvān rules, or says "cannot be determined", which is an answer too.
    How a reader is calibrated, and checked againEvery batch carries a few verses with known rulings, hidden among the rest, and a few shown with no suggestion. The agent scores both, every week.The junior vidvān reviews the scores and decides who goes live, who pauses, who reads with a partner.
    The nightly reportReporter writes it: decisions signed and by whom, headwords closed, escalations open, agreement between readers, how far the frontier moved.The trustees read it. Students see their own names in it.
    Measure it in the Harivaṃśa pilot

    Run half the chapters with agents, half without

    The pilot is 16,100 verses and up to 4,034 headwords in 9 months for ₹37.5 lakh, at about 1,800 verses a month. Nearly all of that is reading time. If the loop is worth building for the full 351,225 verses, the pilot will show it in minutes, not opinions.

    the one numbermin / verseReader and vidvān minutes per signed verse, with agents and without. Today's price assumes about ₹205 a verse.
    Known-answer accuracyHow often students match the hidden rulings, with a suggestion shown and without. If they only agree when shown one, the suggestions are doing the reading, and must be withdrawn.
    Senior's shareThe fraction of verses that reach the senior vidvān. The loop works if it falls and accuracy holds.
    Escalation turnaroundDays from a disagreement to a ruling. This is where projects of this kind usually stall.
    Readers who returnStudents still signing after two months. The note's bet is that seeing their names keeps them.
    Units closedHeadwords and adhyāyas closed per fortnight, so supporters can watch it move.
    How it is wired

    Small parts, most of them already designed

    • Already there: the corpus addressed to the verse across the epic and all eighteen Mahāpurāṇas; the name layer with rival claimants kept as sets; the transcripts of 129 episodes; the atlases; the publish-and-verify step that refuses broken data; the alignment pipeline for recordings.
    • The log is the spine, as the design note has it: decisions append to the corpus's resolution table, signed, and never travel through git. Testimony (Sørensen, Gītā Press, the scan) is never overwritten; rulings supersede rather than delete. A ruling has three outcomes, not two: stands, displaced, or co-stands.
    • Four write surfaces, and everything else is derived: the reading queue on a phone, the senior's gloss, a discussion thread per headword, and code. No published page is edited by hand; if a page is wrong, the decision behind it is wrong.
    • Build order. Two things cannot be retrofitted and come first: the append path from a phone with unreliable data, and the nightly build with its report. At about ten readers, the note says assignment can be a spreadsheet and chasing can be somebody noticing. The agents on this page replace those later, and can be swapped without losing a row.
    • Readers' identity is a pre-signed link per person, as the note settles for a cohort of about ten. No accounts.
    • The readers' side is a phone page that works offline and syncs signed decisions, plus WhatsApp for reminders and voice notes. No reader ever sees GitHub, JSON, or a merge conflict.
    • The agents run as Claude Code in sandboxes, in the style of Sandcastle: one batch or one chapter per run, a review run after each, triggered by webhooks or a schedule.
    • Each repository's CLAUDE.md carries the rules on this page, so every run starts from them: what an agent may not do, how versions are kept apart, when to stop and ask.
    For the team to decide

    Questions before building

    • How is consent for a minor’s name recorded, and by whom: the student, a parent, or the pāṭhaśālā?
    • Which door first? The Bhāgavata holds most of the story and has the most recordings. The Garuḍa Purāṇa already has an atlas, though for its Brahmakāṇḍa, not the Ācārakāṇḍa where the door is.
    • Should two students read every verse, or only the verses an agent marks uncertain? Two readers doubles student cost but gives the agreement figure.
    • When a student is shown a suggestion, how many verses in each batch go blind? A fifth is a starting guess.
    • Which senior vidvān signs for the Harivaṃśa, and how many hours a month can they give? That sets the pace more than anything else.
    • Which upanyāsakars would accept a request to expound a figure's verses, and how is that request made and honoured?
